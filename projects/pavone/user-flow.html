Tint visualiser — customer flow

Every screen below is a real screenshot of the working app on a phone, not a drawing. The before/after is a genuine AI render of that exact car — the same pipeline that runs in production.

1

Land

QR code, ad, or the site's hero button drops them here. One job on this screen: get a photo. No form, no signup, no price — nothing to bounce off.

2

Choose a shade

The five real films, each carrying its QLD legality badge in its own colour — green approves, amber restricts, red warns. The one-tap 'darkest legal combo' does the thinking for people who just want the darkest they're allowed.

3

Wait (≈7–10s)

The most fragile moment in the funnel. Instead of a bare spinner it leads with the heat-rejection argument the brand already sells on, from the first second — so the wait does sales work rather than testing patience.

4

See their own car

The payoff. Same car, same car park, only the glass changed. 'Simulation only' sits under it deliberately — overselling here creates disappointment in the bay.

5

Drag to compare

Dragging is the moment it becomes real — they control the reveal on their own vehicle. This is also the screenshot people send to a partner, which is free distribution.

6

Ask, having earned it

The form only appears after they've seen the result. Four fields, two required. Every earlier step exists to make this one feel small.

7

Two required fields

Name and phone are all that's mandatory — the shop rings back. Vehicle and email are optional so a hesitant customer still converts.

8

Confirm + route

Their render is shown back to them with the next action: book online or call. The lead is already in the shop's hands at this point.

9

Shop side

What Pavone sees: every lead with the exact car and the exact shade they chose — so the callback opens with 'saw you were looking at 20% on the Camry' instead of 'how can I help?'

On the imagery: screens 4 and 5 replay a render this pipeline actually produced (sedan @ 20% VLT, gemini-3.1-flash-image) rather than generating a fresh one — the Gemini account's prepay credits ran out during testing. The pixels are real model output, not a mockup.

Honesty is built in: the "Simulation only" line sits under every result, legality badges are colour-coded by severity, and privacy claims were deliberately toned down (50% VLT reads "slight privacy", not "medium") so nobody is disappointed in the bay.
